**Let Scripture Speak**

---

## God Often Begins with Questions

In Scripture, God does not always give the answer first.

He often asks first.

> “Where are you?” (Genesis 3:9)
> “Where is Abel your brother?” (Genesis 4:9)
> “What are you doing here?” (1 Kings 19:9)

He already knows the answer.

Yet He still asks,
because the person has not yet faced themselves.

In Job, God even asks a long series of questions:

> “Where were you when I laid the foundation of the earth?” (Job 38:4)

Not to overwhelm the person,
but to help them see:

- human limits
- God’s vastness

---

## The Lord Jesus Leads People into Truth through Questions

The Lord Jesus rarely rushed to a conclusion.

He asked:

> “Who do you say that I am?” (Matthew 16:15)
> “Do you want to be healed?” (John 5:6)
> “What do you want me to do for you?” (Luke 18:41)

He was not testing people,
but inviting a response.

He also asked:

> “Have you not read?” (Matthew 12:3)

Not to add more discussion,
but to bring people back to Scripture.

He even changed the question itself:

> “Who is my neighbor?” (Luke 10:29)
> “Which of these three, do you think, was neighbor to him?” (Luke 10:36)

Not to give a definition,
but to turn the perspective.

---

## Apostles and Prophets Also Guide through Questions

When Paul wrote his letters, he kept asking:

> “What then shall we say to these things?” (Romans 8:31)
> “Is there injustice on God’s part?” (Romans 9:14)
> “Are we to continue in sin that grace may abound?” (Romans 6:1)

These questions are not doubt,
but guidance.

The prophets did the same:

> “He looked for it to yield grapes, but it yielded wild grapes.” (Isaiah 5:1–4)

Questions are not only expression,
but a way into the heart.

---

## Questions Are a Path into Truth

From Old Testament to New:

- God
- the Lord Jesus
- the prophets
- the apostles

We see one consistent pattern:

> In Scripture we see that God not only declares,
> but also leads people into truth through questions.

---

## Yet People Respond in Different Ways

Scripture shows us two different spiritual postures.

---

### The Bereans (Acts 17:10–11)

They heard the message,
did not rush to accept it,
and did not easily dismiss it either.

They did something simple yet rare:

> They received the word with all eagerness, examining the Scriptures daily to see if these things were so. (Acts 17:11)

Their distinction was not being “smarter,”
but:

- returning to Scripture
- searching
- discerning

---

### The Church in Corinth

They too were rich in spiritual resources:

- gifts in abundance (1 Corinthians 1:5–7; 12:4–11)
- much knowledge

Yet at the same time:

- divisions (1 Corinthians 1:10–12)
- factions (1 Corinthians 3:3–4)
- spiritual immaturity (1 Corinthians 3:1–3)

They were eager to express themselves,
but did not necessarily stop to discern; (1 Corinthians 14:26–33)

They had much,
but were not necessarily rooted in Scripture. (1 Corinthians 4:6)

---

## This Is Not the Difference between Two Churches

It is the difference between two paths:

### One path:

- return to Scripture
- search
- let Scripture judge everything

(Acts 17:11)

### Another path:

- have much
- experience much
- yet lack a foundation

(1 Corinthians 3:1–3)

---

> The issue is not whether we have experience,
> but whether—
> we let Scripture become the standard by which everything is judged. (1 Corinthians 4:6)

---

## Perhaps the Problem Is Not That We Have Not Learned Enough

But whether—
**we have truly entered into Scripture.**

We live in an age of extraordinary abundance of information.

Content about the Bible is never lacking:

- sermons
- commentaries
- summaries

We can listen to much,
know much,
even talk much.

And still:

> be familiar with content,
> yet never enter Scripture itself.

---

## What We Are Losing Is Not Knowledge

But—entry

Often when we read Scripture, we:

- quickly get an answer
- accept a ready-made understanding
- stop in someone else’s language

In our view, the path of reading Scripture is:

> enter first,
> then see.

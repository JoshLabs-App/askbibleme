# Returning to God’s Word

## A Reading Plan

> “But his delight is in the law of the Lord,
> and on his law he meditates day and night.”
> — Psalm 1:2

> “Man shall not live by bread alone,
> but by every word that comes from the mouth of God.”
> — Matthew 4:4

When many people begin reading Scripture,
they hope for:

* to know God more;
* to draw near to God more;
* to read through the whole Bible with seriousness.

But over time,
reading easily becomes:

* checking a box;
* a task;
* progress tracking;
* pressure.

Then:

* falling behind brings guilt;
* stopping leads to giving up;
* not finishing feels like failure.

We believe the deeper purpose of reading Scripture
has never been only:

# “finishing the reading.”

In our view, God’s word
is not mainly for adding religious knowledge,

but for:

# leading people back into a loving relationship with God.

> “You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind.”
> — Matthew 22:37

Therefore,

what we hope to build
is not a plan to:

# “finish the Bible quickly,”

but a rhythm of:

# long-term, steady, real walking with God.

---

# Our Approach

## 1. A Three-Cycle System

### Addressing: long-term imbalance, fatigue, and easy interruption in Bible reading

Many people fail at reading Scripture
not because they do not love God,

but because over time they read only one kind of content.

For example:

* reading Old Testament continuously becomes tiring;
* reading Law continuously becomes heavy;
* reading only familiar passages leads to stagnation.

So we use a pattern of:

* Old Testament
* New Testament
* Wisdom literature

all at the same time.

The Old Testament helps us know God’s plan and foundation;

the New Testament continually brings life back to Christ and the gospel;

Wisdom literature helps us pray, meditate, and walk with God in real life.

---

## 2. No Check-Ins, No Racing Ahead

### Addressing: guilt, pressure, and giving up

The problem with many reading plans is this:

once you fall behind,

people begin to:

* feel anxious;
* catch up frantically;
* feel they have failed;
* finally stop altogether.

Then reading slowly becomes:

* a KPI;
* a task;
* a discipline exam.

We believe what matters more
is not:

# “whether you kept a perfect streak”

but:

# “whether you keep coming back.”

> “Draw near to God, and he will draw near to you.”
> — James 4:8

So we do not emphasize:

* check-ins;
* consecutive days;
* completion rate;
* reading speed.

Because we believe what truly matters
is not performance,

but relationship.

---

## 3. No Catch-Up—Keep Moving Forward

### Addressing: defeat after interruption

Once many people stop for:

* three days;
* a week;
* a month;

they feel:

“I can’t catch up anymore.”

Then they give up completely.

So our principle is simple:

# do not catch up—just continue.

Because spiritual life is not an exam.

No one needs to make up “missed devotion”
before they can draw near to God again.

We believe what matters more is:

# starting again today.

---

## 4. It Is Fine Even If You Do Not Finish Today’s Plan

### Addressing: spiritual pressure from perfectionism

Sometimes:

* very busy;
* very tired;
* low mood;
* not in a good state;

if you feel:

“I did not finish my three chapters today,”

failure easily follows.

Then you may not want to read even one chapter.

So we emphasize:

# reading only Psalms today is also good.

# reading only the New Testament today is also fine.

Because we believe the more important goal of reading
is not:

# “completing the plan”

but:

# “staying in God’s word.”

Sometimes:

one verse
matters more than three chapters read quickly.

---

# Our Reading Recommendation

We recommend:

## A Three-Cycle Reading System

Each day:

| Type | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Old Testament | 1 chapter (excluding Wisdom books) |
| New Testament | 1 chapter |
| Wisdom | 1 psalm / 1 chapter |

The three tracks cycle independently;
when finished, begin again.

---

## Old Testament Cycle

Scope:

* Genesis → Malachi
  (excluding Job, Psalms, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, Song of Solomon)

The Old Testament helps us know:

* God’s plan;
* history;
* Law;
* the prophets;
* promise.

---

## New Testament Cycle

Scope:

* Matthew → Revelation

Jesus came
not to abolish the Law,

but:

# to fulfill the Law.

> “Do not think that I have come to abolish the Law or the Prophets; I have not come to abolish them but to fulfill them.”
> — Matthew 5:17

And the heart of the Law
is not rules,

but:

# love.

> “You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind.
> This is the great and first commandment.
> And a second is like it: You shall love your neighbor as yourself.
> On these two commandments depend all the Law and the Prophets.”
> — Matthew 22:37–40

Therefore,

the New Testament continually helps us:

* return to Christ;
* return to grace;
* return to the gospel;
* return to a relationship of love.

---

## Wisdom Literature Cycle

Scope:

* Job
* Psalms
* Proverbs
* Ecclesiastes
* Song of Solomon

Wisdom literature helps us:

* pray;
* meditate;
* worship;
* face real life.

They are not theory,

but:

# the honest voice of walking with God.

---

# In Closing

Our deepest goal

is not to help people:

# “read through the whole Bible quickly”

but to help people:

# return again and again to God’s word.

Even if you have stopped for a long time,

today,
you can still begin again.

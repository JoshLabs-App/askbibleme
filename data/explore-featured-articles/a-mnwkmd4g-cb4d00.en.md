# Bible Reading

## Preface: The Problems and Challenges We Face

- Bible reading feels dry; it is hard to find a path of learning and receiving from Scripture
- Time is full, yet little remains for rooting ourselves in God’s word
- It is easy to learn from what others have already summarized—topical content, devotional takeaways
 - because it is simple and clear
 - yet hard to learn from Scripture itself
 - when the one teaching has received something wrongly, we are led wrongly too

---



## I. Models of Bible Study in Scripture

### 1. The Old Testament Pattern (Nehemiah 8:8)

> “They read from the book, from the Law of God, clearly, and they gave the sense, so that the people understood the reading.”

#### Three steps:

1. **Read**
 - People: encounter God’s word
 - Holy Spirit: draw us in

2. **Explain**
 - People: observe and understand
 - Holy Spirit: guard against drift

3. **Understand**
 - People: enter into truth
 - Holy Spirit: open the eyes of the heart

> In our view, true “understanding” is not only worked out by analysis; it is also opened

---

### 2. Jesus’ Example (Luke 24)

The way of study on the road to Emmaus:

1. **Ask questions first**
 - “What are you discussing?”
 - guide observation / reveal where people are

2. **Listen to their understanding (even when it is wrong)**
 - receive their understanding / prepare the heart

3. **Explain from all of Scripture**
 - from Moses to the prophets
 - truth enters in

4. **Let their hearts be opened**
 - the Holy Spirit brings light

#### Summary:
> not giving the answer directly
> but: guide + explain + illuminate

We believe the goal of Bible study is not only to “explain clearly,” but to have hearts set on fire

---

### 3. The Bereans’ Example (Acts 17:10–15)

- hear the word
- search the Scriptures daily to verify
- participate actively

#### Correspondence:
- People: search daily, verify actively
- Holy Spirit: help people see, give inner confirmation

> In our view, Bible study is not only passive listening, but active verification

## 17:11
> They received the word with all eagerness, examining the Scriptures daily to see if these things were so.

---

### 4. Paul’s Disciple-Making Pattern

1. Teach truth (2 Timothy 2:2)
2. Live as an example (1 Corinthians 11:1)
3. Entrust the mission (2 Timothy 2:2)

#### Summary:
- Bible study is not only knowledge
- but the reproduction of life

Not forming “good Christians,” but reproducible disciples

---
## II. Asking the Holy Spirit to Join Our Reading

> In our view, reading Scripture is not only “understanding a book,” but in the Holy Spirit, letting God speak to us

### 1. The Holy Spirit Opens Understanding
- Luke 24
 - Jesus explained the Scriptures
 - the disciples said: “Were not our hearts burning?”
 - “Their eyes were opened”

### 2. The Holy Spirit Teaches and Reminds
- John 14
 - the Spirit will teach you
 - and bring to your remembrance all that I have said to you
 - teaching
 - reminding

### 3. The Holy Spirit Guides into Truth
- John 16
 - the Spirit will guide you into all the truth

- Key understanding:
 - guidance is not a one-time telling, but a process of leading
 - reading Scripture is not finished once, but continually led deeper
 - “hearts burning → eyes opened”
 - understanding Scripture is not only grasping information, but being opened

### 4. The Holy Spirit Enables Receiving Spiritual Things
- 1 Corinthians 2
 - the natural person does not accept the things of the Spirit of God
 - only the spiritual person judges all things

- Key:
 - Scripture is not ordinary text
 - if it feels “ordinary, without impact,” often what is missing is the Spirit’s opening

### 5. The Holy Spirit Illuminates
- Ephesians 1
 - ask that the eyes of your heart may be enlightened

- not:
 - logical understanding alone
- but:
 - spiritual sight

> Sometimes the same verse suddenly “has light”—that is illumination

### 6. The Holy Spirit Makes the Word Life
- John 6
 - “The words that I have spoken to you are spirit and life”

- Scripture is not knowledge, but a carrier of life
- the Spirit makes it “come alive”

---

### Summary: The Work of the Holy Spirit
**Opening, teaching, reminding, guiding, illuminating**

- We believe the Spirit usually does not speak by bypassing Scripture
- but speaks through Scripture itself
- this helps us avoid being misled (for the devil is the father of lies)

---

### Practical Exercise

- Before reading:
 > Lord, by your Spirit open me, that I may understand your word

- While reading:
 - go slower
 - notice repetition
 - notice what touches you

- After reading:
 - what does this passage mean for me today?

## III. Small Group Bible Study in Practice

### 1. Opening
- hearts opened

---

### 2. Read Scripture
- let Scripture speak first

---

### 3. Observation (without explanation)

- What do you see?
- What repeats?
- What words stand out?
- Is there a word that “jumps out”?

#### Core principle:
- use questions to draw out **facts**, not explanation

#### Correct flow:
> ask → listen → amplify → ask again → summarize → go deeper

gather → amplify → organize

---

### 4. Interpretation (truth seen)

#### Common questions:
1. What is God doing in this passage?
2. Why did the people act this way?
3. What is this passage emphasizing?
4. Why did the author record this?
5. How does it relate to context and to all of Scripture?

#### Steps:
1. gather from observation
2. ask the big questions first
3. then ask specific questions
4. the leader organizes, but does not supply all the answers

#### Key reminders:
- guide direction, but do not preach
- without the Spirit → it easily becomes human understanding
- do not only explain “what people did”
- always see “what God is doing”

---

### 5. Application (being illuminated)

- must be concrete
- how does this affect me this week?
- what will I change?

the Spirit illuminates our real condition

## Ephesians 1
> enlighten the eyes of your heart

> “This passage is speaking about me”

#### Key:
- without application = knowledge accumulation only

---

### 6. Prayer (the Spirit internalizes)

> In our view, Bible study is not only the flow of information, but the Spirit at work

---

## IV. Key Principles for Leaders

### 1. Identity: guide, not lecturer
- wrong: keep talking
- right: keep asking

> In our view, what changes people is not me, but the Spirit

---

### 2. Questions matter more than answers
- open questions
- discussable
- guide thinking

through questions, let the Spirit speak

---

### 3. Allow silence
- do not rush to fill the space
- time to think = spiritual space
- give the Spirit time to work

---

### 4. Keep the group on track
- one sentence to return:
 > let us return to Scripture itself

---

### 5. Balance

- method only → rational but without life
- waiting for feeling only → no reading, no observation

- right:
 > read Scripture in the way Jesus modeled, and be opened by the Spirit
